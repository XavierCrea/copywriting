# Make an emotional turn visible | Meeting your firstborn child

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-vgc-meeting-your-firstborn-child.md) · Writing exercise

## Source observations
The author compares an AI-generated paragraph with his own, both 88 words. His version moves from concern about a quiet newborn through a nurse’s explanation to eye contact and a greeting. Dialogue carries the change.

## Interpretation and application
At a fixed length, a moment in which understanding changes can carry more meaning than general declarations of feeling. Use when a story names joy or emotion but does not develop it.

1. Identify the person’s initial question or misunderstanding.
2. Show the action or dialogue that changes it.
3. End on the resulting response and check length.

Check that the emotional turn has a visible cause and that dialogue has records or participant confirmation.

## Limits and results
This is a single writing demonstration without model/version details, controlled conditions, or reader testing. It does not establish future AI capability. Factual stories require factual material.

Evidence status: Effectiveness unknown.

## English writing craft

**Original practice:** “‘Will it fit?’ She slid the case under the seat. ‘We can keep it with us.’” Verify the actual seat and case dimensions.

The question creates uncertainty; the physical verb “slid” resolves it; the final line expresses its practical meaning. Each sentence has a different job. In the source, the short greeting “Hello, son” similarly arrives after a change in understanding rather than announcing emotion at the outset.
