# Sales letters: give the reader a reason to reply

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-bly-sales-letter-brochure-reply.md) · Book case and author account

**Source:** Robert W. Bly, *The Copywriter’s Handbook*, third edition, Chinese translation. Chapter 7, PDF pp. 91–102, especially pp. 93–95 and 100–102.

## What Bly shows

The opening for a Beethoven collection presents the listening offer. Bly reasons that this audience already knows why it might want the music. A letter from a life insurer instead offers a useful guide. Its immediate job is to earn a response from a reader who could use that information. An *Inc.* subscription letter starts with an entrepreneur’s account and turns it into a question its readers might face.

Bly also gives different jobs to the letter, booklet, and reply card. A booklet can hold a diagram, demonstration, or detailed information. The letter tells the reader why any of it is worth their time and how to respond. One reply card gives readers a way to indicate that the timing is wrong.

## How to use it

1. Identify the next useful decision. A buyer who already wants the product can assess a concrete offer. A buyer checking fit needs a fact, drawing, or answer they can use.
2. Let the opening earn attention through that decision. Use a question the reader asked, a development you can document, or a brief situation with a clear connection to the message.
3. Explain why the attachment matters and how to use it. Ask one question the reader can answer, or give them another simple way to state their timing.

## English writing craft

**Original exercise.** At a show, a buyer asked whether a benchtop labeler could sit against a wall. A verified installation diagram gives its footprint and side clearance.

> **Generic:** Just following up on the catalog I sent. Let me know if you have any questions.
>
> **Specific:** You asked whether the labeler could sit against a wall. The attached drawing shows the footprint and the side clearance. Which side would face the wall?

The opening refers to a question the buyer actually asked in the exercise. *Footprint* and *side clearance* tell the reader what they can find in the drawing. The closing gives the reader one choice to report. Drafts built from an incomplete brief should keep unsupported details out and show what to confirm first.

## Fit and evidence

Bly’s source examples are direct mail. The English exercise applies the division of labor to a trade-show email. Keep the relationship and the offer truthful. An attachment, reply, or real deadline must be available before a message promises it. The exercise has no measured results.
