# Dove: keep the benefit consistent while letting the creative change

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-dove-positioning-continuity.md) · Source case and author/publisher account

- **Context:** a new product needs a meaningful difference from traditional soap and continuity across campaigns.
- **Reader:** women experiencing dry skin, as positioned at the time.
- **Task:** make cleansing with moisturization perceptible through varied executions.

## What the material shows

- PDF p. 17 defines positioning through whom the product serves and what it does. An ad crosses out ordinary soap and promises moisturization while bathing.
- The translation on p. 252 introduces obsolescence of traditional soap and claims one-quarter cleansing cream, softness, smoothness, and freedom from soap-related dryness.
- The bath-and-telephone execution on p. 83 uses intimate conversation and a first-use account: cream, lather, cleansing, tightness, down-and-silk sensations, and an easy-to-grip shape. It retains the moisturizing promise at the bottom.
- A separate brand box covers buying, lather in hard water, lack of bathtub residue, and a refund guarantee; p. 261 supplies the appendix translation.
- On p. 17 the author says the positioning still works 25 years later; p. 23 describes continuity of the non-drying promise since 1955. Both are historical retrospectives.

## How the writing works

Positioning becomes a criterion for creative decisions. The reader, bathing situation, dryness problem, and desired result give the one-quarter-cream feature a purpose: explaining how skin feels after washing. The comparison shifts from cleanliness alone to what follows cleansing.

The early execution establishes a reason to switch through visual contrast and ingredient explanation. The bath-and-telephone version invites readers to overhear excitement, then experience the benefit through a character. Formula awareness leads to described sensation. This is an advertising narrative, not verified user research. Lather, creaminess, tactile comparisons, and grip turn an abstraction into bodily actions and impressions.

The story does not carry all the proof. The separate brand box supplies conditions, purchase information, and a guarantee; the ending returns to moisturization. Core benefit and explanatory logic hold the series together while openings and scenes vary. Establish a supported benefit brief, explore several entrances, then check that each returns to the same buying reason.

## Writing steps to borrow

1. Specify the audience, setting, problem, and deliverable result.
2. Select a product reason and express its perceptible consequence.
3. Try direct benefit, product comparison, and character-scene openings around one benefit.
4. Put experience in the story and accountable facts, conditions, and action in clear positions.
5. Compare versions for consistency of the benefit readers take away.

## Draft checks

- Does the creative say whom it helps and how, beyond mood?
- Are formulation, sensation, and promise connected explicitly?
- Without the image is there still a buying reason; without the explanation is the scene intelligible?
- Is scripted dialogue being mislabeled as customer testimony?
- Do executions clarify one promise rather than diverge?

## Suitable tasks

- An important stable benefit has formulation, design, or service support.
- A series needs creative variety without changing its central value every time.

## Transfer limits

- Historical formulation, effects, and audience do not automatically describe today's Dove or another product.
- Intimate conversation is contextual, not a universal tone.
- Continuity must be reconsidered against new product and customer evidence.

## Reported results and evidence

The 25-year and post-1955 continuity accounts lack independently checkable execution-level sales, test groups, or long-term causal data. Cream content, experience, and guarantee are historical ad claims.

Evidence status: Results are reported or relayed by the source; not independently verified.

## English writing craft

**Original exercise for a fictional adjustable lamp, given verified dimming:** direct benefit—“Set the light for the page.” Scene-led—“One more chapter. A little less light.”

The noun *page* anchors both to reading; the second uses parallel fragments to give the adjustment a moment. Different entrances retain the same function. Neither implies reduced eye strain or improved sleep.
