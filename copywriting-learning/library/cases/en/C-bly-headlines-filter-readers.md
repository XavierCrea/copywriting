# Headlines that help the right readers recognize themselves

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-bly-headlines-filter-readers.md) · Book case and author account

**Source:** Robert W. Bly, *The Copywriter’s Handbook*, third edition, Chinese translation. Chapter 2, PDF pp. 26–37. Examples discussed below appear on pp. 27–30 and 34–37.

## What Bly shows

- A Hellmann’s mayonnaise ad opens with a question about baking a cake. The problem selects people who care about the result and gives them a reason to keep reading.
- The headline for industrial mixing equipment lets a large ratio first sound like odds. The body gives the number its engineering meaning. The wordplay leads the intended reader toward the application.
- In a dental-brace brief, Bly lists words for the material and what it can do. Several ideas come from combining those words. He reports that the client used one of the resulting headlines. The book does not supply performance figures or a controlled comparison.

## How to use it

1. Write down the task the headline should help the reader recognize. Look for a product name, problem, or action that makes that task clear.
2. Draft a plain line with one supported benefit or reason to read. Use it to check that the reader and claim will survive a more inventive version.
3. Try a second line with tension, rhythm, or a double meaning that grows from the product detail. Make its practical meaning easy to recover from the image or the next line.

## English writing craft

**Original exercise.** Maintenance staff can slide the drive roller out through the side of a Model A conveyor without removing the belt.

> **Direct:** Replace the drive roller without removing the belt.
>
> **Rhythmic:** The roller slides out. The belt stays on.

The first line names the object, action, and restriction. The second gives two short clauses parallel weight and places *out* and *on* at the ends. It works where the product is already identified. State the Model A limit beside the claim.

## Fit and evidence

Use this for a headline, subject line, or paid placement with a specific reading job. Bly’s 4U review can help reveal a vague claim. A real deadline or need should support any urgency. The source gives writing examples and the author’s account. Results for the exercise are unknown.
