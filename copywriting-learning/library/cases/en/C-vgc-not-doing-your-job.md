# Let researched material form new connections | Not doing your job

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-vgc-not-doing-your-job.md) · Source case and author/publisher account

## Source observations
A *Mad Men* scene introduces the author’s account of creativity: accumulate knowledge and experience, then allow a change of activity to help unexpected connections emerge.

## Interpretation and application
Use when research is sufficient but headlines repeat the same idea. Leave the draft with a defined problem, then test whether new associations serve the benefit. If evidence is missing, research first.

1. List the reader’s question, three verified facts, and the blockage.
2. Try another analogy or let a human writer switch activities briefly.
3. Generate connections and discard those requiring invented facts.

Check that the association rests on confirmed material and still communicates product value.

## Limits and results
The TV scene and commentary do not substantiate neuroscience or productivity gains. A model can deliberately reframe and recombine information; that does not imply human-like subconscious incubation while resting.

Evidence status: Effectiveness unknown.

## English writing craft

**Original practice:** “Your desk, packed for the next stop.” This portable-office-bag idea requires verified capacity and usage conditions.

“Desk” compresses a familiar working setup into one concrete noun. “Packed” connects that setup to mobility, while “next stop” supplies the travel context. The metaphor earns its place only if the product can carry the essentials the reader associates with that desk.
