# Blackmer pumps: make the case for one application

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-bly-blackmer-application-fit.md) · Book case and author account

**Source:** Robert W. Bly, *The Copywriter’s Handbook*, third edition, Chinese translation. Chapter 4, PDF pp. 51–66. The Blackmer example is on p. 56.

## What Bly shows

The ad resembles a page from a directory of pump suppliers. Blackmer is circled. The headline asks readers when it makes sense to call this particular manufacturer. The body acknowledges that many applications offer no special advantage for Blackmer, then names narrower jobs such as handling viscous liquids and slurry. It offers technical literature for readers who want to check the claims. Bly relays a favorable assessment from the marketer. The book does not supply the underlying tests, response rates, or a comparison.

## How to use it

1. Identify the condition that changes a recommendation. It might be the material, operating environment, physical fit, or supply arrangement.
2. Use the narrowest scope that still matters to the buyer. Check whether the evidence is for that use. State the limits of any test or comparison.
3. Give the buyer something they can check, such as a model-specific data sheet, a test setup, or an application guide.

## English writing craft

**Original exercise.** A fictional benchtop labeler has been tested on batches of 100 to 300 paper labels. A setup sheet is available. Other stocks have not been tested.

> **Broad:** A better labeling solution for every production line.
>
> **Application-led:** For short runs of paper labels.  
> Tested in batches of 100 to 300. Ask for the setup sheet to check your label stock.

*For* gives the short line a selection job. *Short runs* describes the task, and *paper* keeps the claim in scope. The next sentence lets a reader check whether “short” covers their work. The setup sheet provides a practical next step without implying every label will fit.

## Fit and evidence

Use this when readers need to match a product to their circumstances, especially when competing offers sound alike. A shared feature can still be relevant. Any claim to an exclusive feature or better comparative performance needs its own evidence.
