# Magic Stat: clear the installation barrier, then put the technology into a daily routine

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-sugarman-magicstat-objection-sequence.md) · Source case and author/publisher account

- **Context:** the Magic Stat programmable thermostat in a JS&A mail-order ad.
- **Reader:** household buyers seeking comfort and savings but concerned about installation, setup, and reliability.
- **Task:** move from doubts about appearance and name to an understanding of practical value.
- **Medium:** full Chinese ad translation, original-ad thumbnail, and author commentary.

## What the material shows

- On p. 225 the headline, caption, and opening criticize the name, plastic appearance, and missing digital display; a subheading promises a surprise.
- The first positive discovery is easy installation. The author identifies wiring risk and hiring cost as major barriers. Six daily settings and seven-day programming are explained through waking, work, dinner, television, and sleep.
- The copy contrasts starting at a time with reaching a temperature by that time in changing weather, then discusses setup, memory, power loss, manufacturer stability, warranty, and payback.
- Page 227 offers the historical $79 standard and $99 better-looking casing options, $4 postage, and a one-year return period. It closes by ranking comfort and savings above looks and name.

## How the writing works

Acknowledging visible weaknesses can make the writer sound like someone choosing on the buyer's behalf. The promised discovery earns continuation. But the first reversal resolves an exclusion condition: while wiring or hiring worries remain, savings may seem irrelevant. Order evidence by the seriousness of the barrier rather than mechanically criticizing before praising.

Programming capacity becomes a routine readers can mentally try. Weather differences give adaptive preheating a meaningful comparison. Manufacturer and guarantee answer long-term dependence; price and risk terms address payment. The product has not become prettier; the criteria for evaluating it have shifted toward comfort and savings.

## Writing steps to borrow

1. Rank concerns as purchase blockers, understanding needs, and payment justification.
2. Use a contrast opening only when it reflects the reader's view; deliver a supported discovery promptly.
3. Translate parameters into use moments, inputs, actions, and results.
4. Compare with a familiar existing method so the difference solves an understood problem.
5. Supply reliability and deliverable guarantees before the offer; return to the central benefit.

## Draft checks

- Does the first positive fact remove the largest barrier?
- Does the scene explain a function rather than rename it with an adjective?
- Are minor weaknesses honestly retained?
- Have historical installation, savings, or tax claims become current promises?

## Suitable tasks

- A visible appearance or naming weakness coexists with supported functional advantages and resolvable setup barriers.

## Transfer limits

- 24 volts, live wiring, up to 30% savings, a 15% tax allowance, one-year payback, and returns are historical claims, not current facts or installation advice.
- Historical superlatives illustrate tone; present performance and service need their own support.

## Reported results and evidence

The author says the ad ran about three years from 1983; p. 228 says more than three. He reports brand success and later acquisition by Honeywell. Record roughly three years or longer as his account, without original placement, sales, or acquisition records and without attributing acquisition to copy.

Evidence status: Results are reported or relayed by the source; not independently verified.

## English writing craft

**Original contrast exercise:** “Warm at seven—not just switched on at seven.”

Repeating *at seven* holds time constant while contrasting an experienced result with a device action. The wording is useful only when the current product can deliver that distinction under stated conditions. It is not a recovered line from the ad.
