# Make meaning catch up with a polished draft | Writing, slow

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-vgc-writing-slow.md) · Writing exercise

## Source observations
Through handwriting and one-finger phone typing, the author argues that physical friction can encourage attention to individual words and prevent a polished appearance from masking unfinished thought.

## Interpretation and application
For AI-assisted copy, pause after generation to explain each claim, its evidence, and its connection to neighboring sentences. Simulating slow input does not itself improve thought. Use when smooth prose contains vague claims or logical jumps.

1. State the paragraph’s purpose in one sentence before expanding it.
2. Mark each claim, supporting evidence, and connection.
3. Refine the vaguest term, then read aloud to check meaning.

Check the basis for key judgments and whether fluency hides a causal gap.

## Limits and results
Handwriting is optional. Deadlines, physical abilities, and workflows differ; judge clarity rather than speed. The source provides experience and cited opinion, not comparative data on writing speed and quality.

Evidence status: Effectiveness unknown.

## English writing craft

**Original practice:** Abstract draft: “Enjoy effortless organization.” Revision: “Keep receipts in one pocket and tickets in the other.” Verify the two-pocket structure and intended uses.

The revision replaces an adjective with two objects and their locations. Parallel “in one … in the other” makes the arrangement easy to picture. The extra words do useful work: they explain the promised organization instead of merely asserting it.
