# The changed car quote: how a preliminary choice acquires its own supporting reasons

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-cialdini-car-quote-changed.md) · Source case and author/publisher account

- **Context:** the author observes Chevrolet dealers while posing as a sales trainee: a low quote wins a decision, then its advantage is withdrawn.
- **Reader:** shoppers comparing cars and offers before signing.
- **Task:** the historical tactic seeks completion after removing the initial incentive.
- **Medium:** quotes, forms, loan discussions, test drives, and closing talk.

## What the material shows

- On PDF p. 142, a quote is 400 dollars below competitors, with no intent to close at that price; it first secures the decision to buy there.
- Forms, financing discussion, and sometimes a one-day drive shown to colleagues or neighbors give buyers time to discover further reasons for their choice.
- Pages 142–143 add back the 400 through a missing air-conditioning charge, bank arithmetic error, or manager refusal. The salesperson emphasizes parity with competitors and the buyer's own selection.
- A trade-in variant overvalues the old car before a manager lowers it to the blue-book level. One buyer still signs and apologizes for trying to obtain an unfair advantage.
- Pages 141 and 144 explain that commitment can acquire new support, surviving withdrawal of its initial incentive. The author acknowledges it does not work on everyone.

## How the writing works

The mechanism goes beyond an initial yes. The incentive wins comparison, while forms, finance, and experience move the buyer into imagining ownership and organizing additional reasons. Those reasons may independently sustain the choice after price returns to market level. Reducing the explanation to wasted time misses self-persuasion.

Explanations involving mistakes or management deflect responsibility; market-price comparisons reduce the salience of added cost; reminding buyers of their choice returns attention to commitment. Participation can generate reasons in the buyer's own mind. A constructive application provides complete, deliverable terms while helping customers identify use needs, verify benefits, and connect their own confirmed reasons to evidence. Deceptive quoting is not necessary to do that. Material changes require a fresh comparison, not reliance on earlier assent.

## Writing steps to borrow

1. Establish complete prices, mandatory fees, specifications, delivery, and service terms before customer investment.
2. Ask meaningful questions about goals, budget, and nonnegotiables; avoid questions engineered only for agreement.
3. Let buyers test a key fit reason and describe the experience in their own words.
4. Present confirmed needs, observed fit, configuration and price, unresolved issues, and next step.
5. Explain any changed conditions and their impact separately; assess valid inquiries/purchases and regret or cancellation, not early commitment alone.

Proposal structure: desired result → personally verified fit → configuration and complete terms → unresolved trade-offs → freely chosen next step.

## Draft checks

- Are reasons grounded in the buyer's needs and experience or written for them by the seller?
- Is the initial price obtainable, with mandatory charges visible?
- Are preference and purchase commitment distinguished?
- Does product fit remain when the incentive is removed?
- Do price changes leave practical exit and alternative options?

## Transfer limits

- Relevant need, autonomous choice, and useful experience matter; do not assume the mechanism without them.
- The goal is genuine buyer reasoning, not preserving an incorrect decision through hidden charges.
- Process friction, price anchoring, and other influences are not excluded.
- Public declarations are unnecessary; high-value and group decisions need room for reconsideration.
- Historical observation does not predict closing rates or validate every associated method.

## Reported results and evidence

The author reports observing the tactic, one continuing buyer, and common dealership use. Sample size, comparative close rates, cancellations, and long-term trust results are not supplied.

Evidence status: Results are reported or relayed by the source; not independently verified.

## English writing craft

**Original proposal transition:** “You confirmed [need]. In the trial, you observed [result]. Here is the configuration and total cost for that use.”

The first two sentences distinguish the customer's words from observed evidence. *Total* belongs only with complete charges. If terms change, name the change directly rather than using earlier agreement as pressure.
