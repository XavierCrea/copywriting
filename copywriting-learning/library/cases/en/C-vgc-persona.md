# Write from one real person’s situation | Person(a)

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-vgc-persona.md) · Source case and author/publisher account

## Source observations
The author connects Schulz’s personal experience with the *Peanuts* characters, advocates studying a real prospective customer, and reports that readers recognize themselves in his own stories.

## Interpretation and application
Use when demographic labels do not help produce a specific sentence. Start with an individual’s language and decision context, then check other comparable material for representativeness.

1. Select a recorded inquiry or buying decision.
2. Describe the task, concern, and customer’s actual words.
3. Write for that person, then check whether similar customers share the concern.

Check that the problem comes from records and that one life story has not been assigned to an entire audience.

## Limits and results
One interview can produce a writing hypothesis, not prove market demand. Preserve meaningful differences between groups. Creative experience and reader resonance are author reports, without sampling research or campaign results.

Evidence status: Effectiveness unknown.

## English writing craft

**Original practice:** “Need to reach your charger before boarding? Keep it in the front pocket.” Verify the situation and pocket fit.

“Before boarding” narrows a general storage benefit to a recognizable moment. The question names a task, and the imperative immediately answers it. Addressing “you” is useful here because the situation is concrete; the pronoun alone would not make generic copy personal.
