# Samples and gifts: distinguish useful experience from an obligation to reciprocate

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-cialdini-samples-and-gifts.md) · Source case and author/publisher account

- **Context:** airport fundraising gifts, supermarket cheese sampling, and Amway's household BUG trial kit; a cola-and-raffle study supplies mechanism background.
- **Reader:** passersby undecided about donating and prospective buyers unfamiliar with a product.
- **Task:** obtain a response to an unfamiliar request; commercially, let buyers experience a product before deciding.
- **Medium:** face-to-face contact, supermarket sampling, home trials, and follow-up.

## What the material shows

- PDF pp. 48–50 relay Regan's study: during an apparent art-rating task, an assistant brings a cola or returns empty-handed, then asks participants to buy raffle tickets. Cola recipients reportedly buy twice as many; liking the assistant is less closely associated with purchasing than in the no-cola condition.
- Pages 51–53 describe Krishna fundraisers giving flowers or books, refusing returns, then seeking donations. The author also describes avoidance, refused gifts, and venue warnings: people learn to change the contact and acceptance stage.
- Pages 55–56 describe samples as both product experience and gifts. An anecdote relayed from Vance Packard's *The Hidden Persuaders* says an Indiana supermarket sold 1,000 pounds of cheese in hours after customers cut their own samples.
- Pages 56–58 describe the BUG kit of household/personal-care products left free for 24, 48, or 72 hours, followed by a visit to take orders. Distributors report increased sales and buyers purchasing, on average, half the items.

## How the writing works

The key sequence is what someone receives before being asked to act. A gift can make a donation partly about repayment rather than support for an unfamiliar organization; the cola study suggests liking alone may not explain that response. Sampling also supplies firsthand experience, replacing reliance on seller assertions. The material does not isolate the contributions of these two mechanisms.

For writing, give a small piece of decision-relevant value and explain a voluntary next step. The home kit makes the sequence concrete: suitable people, a result worth checking, usable products, adequate trial time, and an arranged follow-up. Ask what fitted and what did not before recommending a purchase. Reciprocity is not a substitute for value. Forced gifts and refused returns explain pressure, and recognition of that pressure can cause avoidance before acceptance. A useful sample should still help judgment when no sale follows.

## Writing steps to borrow

1. Identify the question a sample can answer: fit, cleaning procedure, or operating difficulty.
2. State contents, conditions, charges/shipping, trial period, and return requirements; describe an observable task.
3. Include a short use card: where to try, what to observe, what suggests fit, and when to ask for help.
4. Follow up at the agreed time, collect experience, then connect suitable specifications to purchasing or inquiry; preserve the choice not to buy.
5. Track collection, actual use, qualified inquiries, purchases, and returns separately.

Application structure: decision to make → what the sample verifies → trial and conditions → next step based on the result. Supply actual product facts.

## Draft checks

- Can the promised effect appear during the trial?
- Is the sample useful even without purchase?
- Do free-trial terms match the follow-up?
- Does the recommendation use actual experience rather than indebtedness?
- Are units, customer conversion, and revenue distinct?

## Transfer limits

- Use for demonstrable products where trial resolves meaningful uncertainty; unrelated gifts do not establish differentiation.
- Expensive trials, delayed effects, or strict supplier-gift policies require another verification route; these contexts were not tested here.
- Poor experience, hidden charges, repeated pressure, or perceived forced repayment can harm willingness to continue.
- Sidebar expert commentary is distinct from the main author; covert use is not this library's recommendation.
- Historical figures do not forecast a project; attribution of Amway growth does not control other factors.

## Reported results and evidence

The book combines relayed research, author observations, third-party anecdotes, and distributor reports. Twice as many raffle tickets, 1,000 pounds of cheese in hours, and half the BUG items purchased retain their separate sources and measures; they are not one general marketing effect.

Evidence status: Results are reported or relayed by the source; not independently verified.

## English writing craft

**Original trial-copy pattern:** “Try [task] with [sample]. Check [observable result]. Then decide whether [next step] makes sense.”

Imperative verbs make evaluation concrete; *then decide* preserves agency. Place real cost, duration, and return conditions alongside it. This is new wording, not a promise that sampling doubles sales.
