# Find a useful connection by looking longer | Noticing

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-vgc-noticing.md) · Source case and author/publisher account

## Source observations
The lesson uses sustained attention to paintings and repeated viewing of footage to illustrate how initially overlooked relationships become visible. Two paintings support the observation exercise.

## Interpretation and application
Use when a draft offers generic advantages without a distinctive product detail. In our application, a detail must answer a reader’s question; sensory imagination is a research prompt, not evidence about a product.

1. Rewatch a real operation and record actions and obstacles.
2. Connect a detail to the user’s goal, then verify it.
3. Try that detail in a headline or scene opening.

Check which question it answers and distinguish observed facts from imagination.

## Limits and results
The three-hour exercise belongs to its teaching context; research time should fit the task. The lesson offers observational experience, not measured marketing results.

Evidence status: Effectiveness unknown.

## English writing craft

**Original practice, not a source quotation:** “One hand on the handle. One free for the stairs.” Verify the single-handed operation first.

The repeated “One” creates a balanced pair. The first fragment establishes an action; the second names what that action makes possible. “The stairs” gives a concrete context where the freed hand matters. This is stronger than adding an unsupported adjective such as “effortless.”
