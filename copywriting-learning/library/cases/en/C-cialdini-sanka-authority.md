# Sanka: how the speaker’s identity changes the weight of a claim

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-cialdini-sanka-authority.md) · Source case and author/publisher account

- **Context:** actor Robert Young advertises Sanka. Cialdini argues that viewers transfer associations with his long-running Dr. Welby role to advice about decaffeinated coffee.
- **Reader:** American viewers familiar with the character and concerned about coffee and health; actual audience distribution is unspecified.
- **Task:** the ad brings trust in the physician character into product choice.
- **Medium:** television and the small ad reproduction in figure 6-3.

## How the writing works

The face can affect credibility before viewers inspect the claim. Familiarity with a caring physician character may supply medical associations when the actor speaks about caffeine. The connection is role-to-question, not celebrity alone. Real expertise also needs to be recognizable where a relevant claim appears: specify what judgment is required, then identify a qualified person, institution, or report and explain relevant work rather than displaying a title alone.

Competence and truthful presentation are different. Interests, grounds, scope, limits, and product relationships all matter. Turn authority into a traceable reasoning route: who, with which expertise, used what evidence to reach which limited conclusion? Presentation can help identify evidence but cannot manufacture it. The historical character association provides no current health endorsement.

## Writing steps to borrow

1. Make the claim an answerable question about composition, tests, operation, or experience.
2. Verify the speaker's identity, relevant competence, and product relationship.
3. Obtain traceable evidence for the model, version, sample, and conditions; do not expand a test into overall excellence.
4. State relevant competence, basis, conclusion, and scope, with accessible sources.
5. Use authentic work and test processes as visual cues rather than clothing or titles as proof.
6. Check whether readers understand why this claim is credible; recognition or recall is not correct judgment or sales lift.

Endorsement structure: relevant real expertise → checkable evidence/process → specific object and metric → limited conclusion → conditions.

## Draft checks

- Does qualification match this claim?
- Are acting roles, endorsement, and professional identity distinguished?
- Does testing cover this model and statement?
- Is independence affected by payment or other interests?
- Does the evidence survive removal of famous faces, coats, and titles?
- Has historical explanation become current health or composition advice?

## Transfer limits

- Role recognition varies by audience; past US television response is not universal.
- Fame, titles, and marks cannot expand evidential scope.
- This case supports no current caffeine-health, ingredient, or performance advice.
- Repeated placement does not prove causation or enable forecasting.
- Admitting a small drawback does not establish the truth of other claims or justify hiding a material defect.

## Reported results and evidence

Large sales and repeated versions are author reports, explained through physician-role association. Checkable revenue, increment, exact period, and controls are absent.

Evidence status: Results are reported or relayed by the source; not independently verified.

## English writing craft

**Original evidence-first pattern:** “[Named lab] tested [model] under [conditions]. The report records [specific result].”

The subject identifies responsibility; *tested* names the work; *records* attributes the conclusion. Avoid “expert-approved” when the evidence is a limited test rather than an overall endorsement.
