# Practice choosing words for one reader | Good copywriting practice

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-vgc-good-copywriting-practice.md) · Writing exercise

## Source observations
The author replaces undirected morning freewriting with fifteen minutes writing to his son. He reports that writing for a particular reader subsequently feels more natural.

## Interpretation and application
A definite recipient shapes information, tone, and the amount of background needed. This trains reader adaptation rather than the mechanical insertion of “you.” Use when a draft broadcasts to everyone and drifts in tone or emphasis.

1. Choose someone you know and spend fifteen minutes explaining one small thing.
2. Remove background they know; supply what they need to understand.
3. For customer work, replace the practice recipient with verified customer questions.

Check what this reader knows and worries about, and what each paragraph contributes.

## Limits and results
A practice partner’s preferences do not represent buyers. Intimate language does not automatically fit business communication. The outcome is a self-reported change in writing habit, not a controlled experiment or sales result.

Evidence status: Effectiveness unknown.

## English writing craft

**Original practice:** “Maya, start with the side pocket. Your charger fits there, so you can reach it without unpacking.” Bag design and capacity remain assumptions until checked.

The name establishes a recipient, but the useful adaptation is the sequence: location, fit, practical consequence. “So” makes the benefit’s logic explicit. Keep the name only where addressing the person directly is natural; personalization should survive its removal.
