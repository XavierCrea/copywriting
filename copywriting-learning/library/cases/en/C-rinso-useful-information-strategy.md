# Rinso: make the ad useful, then check which benefit it actually teaches

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-rinso-useful-information-strategy.md) · Source case and author/publisher account

- **Context:** household problems motivate reading, but an ad also has a defined brand task.
- **Reader:** someone with a specific stain-removal problem.
- **Task:** connect useful information to product selection and check the value remembered.

## What the material shows

- PDF p. 86 arranges 12 numbered stain photographs in three columns and four rows, with 12 matching instructions. The headline offers stain removal; the subheading names Rinso and directions.
- The opening does mention whiter clothes and SOLIUM before addressing common stains. It attributes tested advice to Lever scientists and first requires washable, colorfast fabric.
- Each entry connects a problem, action, and product. The ending claims a price about 20% below ordinary detergents, explains production cost, offers a quality guarantee, and gives an address for free copies.
- On p. 85 the author reports higher reading and recall than other detergents surveyed, yet questions whether the ad should have been used because it departed from the agreed whiteness strategy. The page does not establish rejection or nonpublication.
- His general claim that useful information attracts over 75% more readers is not a measured lift for this ad.

## How the writing works

The entry point is the reader's stain rather than a brand introduction. Distinct photographs help recognition; numbering takes readers to the answer. The page supports both sequential reading and reference use. Headline, subheading, and instructions move from task to product to action, integrating the brand more closely than a logo attached to general advice.

Attribution, fabric conditions, and differentiated treatments show practical knowledge rather than a universal cleaning claim. The price argument adds another reason to buy and explains the cost difference. Offering copies makes the page itself obtainable. Saving and reuse are plausible, but no retention or repeat-purchase data are given.

The strategy critique is about emphasis, not omission: whiteness and ingredients are present. Yet the headline, dominant images, and 12 entries organize attention around stain removal. One sentence about whiteness need not determine memory. Check what the whole page demonstrates, not merely whether a keyword appears. Preserve usefulness while selecting problems, visuals, steps, and an ending that support the brand task. That is a proposed test direction, not a proven winning rewrite.

## Writing steps to borrow

1. Identify frequent reader problems the product can genuinely help solve.
2. Organize by those problems with navigable images, headings, or an index.
3. Give conditions, actions, and results, showing the product's role.
4. Check which benefit dominates the visuals, headline, and examples together.
5. If off-strategy, reselect problems and evidence while retaining practical usefulness.
6. Measure ease of finding answers, correct benefit recall, inquiries, and purchases separately.

## Draft checks

- Can readers locate their problem quickly?
- If any competing brand could replace this one, should the product's role be clearer?
- Are the instructions and material conditions valid?
- Does the page repeatedly demonstrate its central benefit?
- Are readership, recall, inquiries, and sales recorded separately?

## Suitable tasks

- Readers have operational questions and supported, safe answers are available.
- A reference-style tutorial can support both use and brand choice.

## Transfer limits

- Do not label this independently verified market success or turn criticism into a documented rejection.
- Historical chemical instructions, performance, and pricing require current support before use.
- Judge strategic consistency from dominant meaning, not keyword presence.

## Reported results and evidence

Ogilvy reports superior reading and recall and criticizes strategy; survey definitions, sample, and sales records are unknown. His conditional criticism establishes neither rejection nor deployment; publication status remains unknown here. Over 75% is a general assertion, and 20% an ad price claim, not sales lift.

Evidence status: Results are reported or relayed by the source; not independently verified.

## English writing craft

**Original tutorial-heading exercise:** “Stain removal information” → “Coffee on your cuff? Start here.”

A named substance and location help readers recognize a task; the short direction makes the next action clear. This demonstrates navigation only. Any actual treatment must come from current product and fabric guidance.
