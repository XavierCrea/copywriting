# Nautilus: make reading participatory, then distinguish players from buyers

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-sugarman-nautilus-participation.md) · Source case and author/publisher account

- **Context:** a historical Nautilus equipment promotion.
- **Task:** clear inventory and encourage participation.
- **Medium:** a published direct-response print ad and author retrospective.

## What the material shows

- PDF pp. 253–254 describe inventory and original-price constraints. Starting at $500, the promotion deducts $10 per correctly identified spelling error; the author says there are 25.
- Pages 255–256 give calculation, submission, and shipping rules, including participation without purchase. Product use, requirements, specifications, and trial arrangements remain in the copy.
- The author says most found about 20 errors, profits exceeded expectations, and inventory sold out. Many nonbuyers also spent time reading and contacting the seller. He later used the idea for a spelling machine, without reporting that result.

## How the writing works

Careful reading becomes something readers do for their own benefit. Each discovery has a calculable reward and immediate progress feedback, encouraging repeated inspection. The ad becomes a task environment as well as a seller's statement. Clear rules connect participation to an executable offer.

People can enjoy a puzzle without needing exercise equipment. Reported commercial success and substantial nonbuyer engagement can coexist. Evaluate reading, qualified reach, and profitable transactions separately. A more relevant adaptation makes the activity itself support selection or experience, so reward also brings product understanding. The later spelling-machine reflection suggests this direction but does not experimentally establish it.

## Writing steps to borrow

1. Define the product decision readers need to understand before choosing a task.
2. Make participation yield useful information, such as fit or ranked needs, with a clear reward basis.
3. State eligibility, calculation, deadline, submission, fees, and a worked calculation.
4. Keep a direct learning or buying route for people who skip the activity.
5. Track participation, completion, qualified inquiries, orders, and actual margin separately.

## Draft checks

- Does completion teach the product or just the game?
- Can readers obtain the reward as they understand it?
- Does copy improperly reinterpret existing price or delivery constraints?
- Are engagement and sales conflated, or nonbuyers incorrectly treated as proof of total failure?

## Transfer limits

- Promotion must match actual arrangements; wording cannot override channel pricing agreements.
- Profit from participants missing errors is not a default reusable strategy.
- Interaction does not establish superior function; no comparative result is supplied for the spelling-machine extension.

## Reported results and evidence

Sellout, above-expected profit, and many nonbuying participants are author reports. Original orders, profit accounts, audience size, and a control version are unavailable.

Evidence status: Results are reported or relayed by the source; not independently verified.

## English writing craft

**Original product-selector microcopy:** “Answer three fit questions. See which configuration matches.”

Two action verbs connect effort to a useful output. Use the stated number only if there really are three questions and the selector can support its recommendation. Avoid promising a discount whose conditions are hidden in the activity.
