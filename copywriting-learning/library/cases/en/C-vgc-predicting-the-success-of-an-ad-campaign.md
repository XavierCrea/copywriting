# Diagnose the audience and offer before rewriting | Predicting the success of an ad campaign

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-vgc-predicting-the-success-of-an-ad-campaign.md) · Source case and author/publisher account

## Source observations
An interview explains 40/40/20 as audience, offer, and creative. Audience research includes relevant purchases and channel behavior, not just demographics. The offer includes demand, price, and incentives; creative includes copy, design, and format. A reported failed promotion illustrates why rewriting cannot necessarily repair weak demand.

## Interpretation and application
Use when a poorly performing ad is being treated solely as a headline problem. Check who would buy and why they would accept the transaction before polishing expression.

1. Separate confirmed evidence about buyers, transaction terms, and creative.
2. Isolate a consequential evidence gap and propose a testable hypothesis.
3. Define the metric and test scope, then write a corresponding version.

Check audience fit and the factual basis for price and urgency.

## Limits and results
Treat 40/40/20 as a diagnostic reminder, not measured universal weights or a success-probability formula. The interview offers no validated statistical model; the failure is a participant account without raw data. Scarcity, incentives, and guarantees require real, authorized terms.

Evidence status: Effectiveness unknown.

## English writing craft

**Original practice:** “For teams replacing cracked sample cases: request the hinge-test report before ordering.” Use only with a verified buyer situation and an available report.

The opening qualifies the reader by a concrete problem. The second half offers evidence at the relevant decision point. “Before ordering” makes the low-pressure sequence explicit. Better wording still cannot make an irrelevant product or unavailable report a good offer.
