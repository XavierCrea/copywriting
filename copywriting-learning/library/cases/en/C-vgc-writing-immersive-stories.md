# Build presence with action, dialogue, and restraint | Writing immersive stories

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-vgc-writing-immersive-stories.md) · Writing exercise

## Source observations
Three stories demonstrate facts, actions, and dialogue. The author recommends limiting explanations of inner thoughts and excess background; present tense can make events feel immediate.

## Interpretation and application
Let observable action carry emotion while readers connect it. Omission should remove redundant description, not necessary causality. Use when a case story declares anxiety or satisfaction without showing events.

1. Mark abstract emotions and explanations of thought.
2. Replace one with verified action, dialogue, or bodily response.
3. Keep necessary context; try present tense consistently.

Check whether readers can infer feeling from evidence and whether omissions obscure why events happen.

## Limits and results
The lesson uses “realism” as a teaching term, not a full definition of a literary movement. Inner thought and past tense remain legitimate choices. Never invent actions or quotations in factual customer cases. The source offers demonstrations, not reader experiments or campaign data.

Evidence status: Effectiveness unknown.

## English writing craft

**Original fictional practice:** “The latch clicks. He lifts the case by one handle, pauses, then puts the sample inside.” This is not a real test or testimonial.

“Clicks” contributes sound and a completed action. “Pauses” changes the pace without declaring what the person thinks. The sequence creates presence, but it does not prove satisfaction, durability, or safety. A factual case needs the actual observed sequence.
