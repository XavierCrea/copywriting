# Compress a passage, then audit what was lost | One of the best copywriting exercises

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-vgc-one-of-the-best-copywriting-exercises.md) · Writing exercise

## Source observations
The author recommends reducing an already compact encyclopedia passage to roughly one-third its length, demonstrating 175 words reduced to 51. The rewrite combines funding details and summarizes growth as a multiple. He predicts benefits from regular practice.

## Interpretation and application
A length target forces reorganization, but the example loses detail. Compression may also turn temporal proximity into causality or simultaneity. Use for editing practice or an overlong draft whose core information must remain.

1. List indispensable facts, qualifications, and relationships.
2. Aim for one-third the length by merging repetition.
3. Check omissions, numbers, attribution, and chronology; relax the target if needed.

Check that no new cause has been invented and that the reader’s task and material qualifications survive.

## Limits and results
Legal qualifications, technical specifications, and timelines may resist merging. Fewer words do not guarantee improvement. Before-and-after text is available, but long-term practice benefits are predictions without measured outcomes.

Evidence status: Effectiveness unknown.

## English writing craft

**Original fictional logistics practice:** Draft: “Orders received before noon leave that afternoon. This schedule applies on working days only.” Edit: “On working days, orders received before noon ship that afternoon.”

Moving the condition to the front lets it govern the whole promise. “Ship” replaces “leave” with a more precise action. The edit retains the cutoff and dispatch timing; it does not silently promise same-day delivery. Verify actual service terms before use.
