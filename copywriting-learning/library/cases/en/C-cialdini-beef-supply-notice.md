# Beef supply notices: turn a real change and useful advance information into a purchasing reason

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-cialdini-beef-supply-notice.md) · Source case and author/publisher account

- **Context:** a student of the author who owns a beef-import business has salespeople call existing customers with three information combinations.
- **Reader:** supermarket and other food-retail buyers planning supply and operations.
- **Task:** compare standard information, a shortage notice, and an added exclusive-source explanation.
- **Medium:** sales calls.

## What the material shows

- PDF pp. 336–337 describe the exercise without naming the company or supplying original records.
- Group one hears the standard pitch. Group two also hears that imported beef may be scarce in coming months. Group three additionally hears that the business obtained the information through a special, exclusive source.
- The author describes the third condition as scarcity of both goods and access to information.
- The Chinese edition on p. 337 reports order quantity in group two increased by more than one baseline amount, and group three by five baseline amounts, relative to group one. This note preserves the edition's incremental wording rather than recasting it as total multiples or conversion rates.
- The footnote asserts that the shortage and exclusive source were genuine. That is the author's stated premise, not independent verification here.

## How the writing works

A buyer must assess future continuity as well as product quality. Supply risk makes postponing a purchase less certain; advance information can create a planning advantage. The exclusive-source condition is not simply repetition of the shortage—it suggests that fewer people have the information. Psychological scarcity and practical inventory planning may both contribute. The account separates neither mechanism nor establishes growth in long-term total demand.

Useful urgency comes from a checkable change affecting the buyer's plan. Name the category, period, source, certainty, and options rather than merely urging speed. If a special source is real and disclosable, explain the timing advantage without mystique. Connect the notice to requirements, available batches, or staged delivery, supporting proportionate planning rather than indiscriminate larger orders.

## Writing steps to borrow

1. Verify affected items/specifications, timing, certainty, and update date against supply information.
2. Open with the effect on the buyer's plan, then briefly explain the evidence.
3. Describe genuine exclusive access or advance notice only when it exists and can be disclosed.
4. List deliverable options: quantities, batches, staged supply, or suitable alternatives.
5. Request relevant specifications, amounts, and dates so buyers can reschedule.
6. Track volume, advance ordering, fulfillment, cancellation, stock, and later purchases to distinguish demand growth from timing or supplier shifts; retain notice versions and timestamps.

Notice structure: affected item/window → change and certainty → source/update → planning effect → available delivery options → information needed for a reply.

## Draft checks

- Is current inventory confused with possible future import scarcity?
- Is exclusive information confused with exclusive selling rights?
- Is the information current, with qualifications close to claims?
- Does the next step support actual needs rather than assume larger orders are best?
- Is order quantity kept distinct from customer conversion, order count, and profit?
- Is the follow-up period long enough to detect later reductions after early buying?

## Transfer limits

- Use for real supply changes tied to purchasing tasks and deliverable arrangements; indefinitely replicable supply is different.
- Exclusive information is not automatically credible; unclear or stale sources can undermine trust.
- Short-term volume may reflect stocking earlier or switching suppliers, not total demand or profit growth.
- Professional retail buyers differ from general consumers.
- The incomplete historical design cannot isolate one phrase or support current forecasts.

## English writing craft

**Original notice pattern:** “For [item] needed by [date], please check availability before confirming your schedule. [Verified change and update date]. We can currently offer [actual option].”

Lead with the buyer's timing, preserve uncertainty through *check*, and qualify *currently* with a dated fact. Do not turn an expected shortage into a statement that today's stock is gone.
