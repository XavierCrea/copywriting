# Give an object value through shared memories | A poetic masterpiece

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-vgc-a-poetic-masterpiece.md) · Source case and author/publisher account

## Source observations
A church-organ fundraising letter uses repeated openings and groups of three to connect an instrument with familiar life ceremonies. It turns receiving its gifts into giving back. The author credits a receptive congregation, clear short copy, and emotional rhythm together.

## Interpretation and application
Recall experienced value before asking for action. Repetition should organize real connections, not decorative adjectives. Use when readers share experiences in which the product or organization demonstrably participated.

1. List three concrete occasions of use.
2. Connect different values with a repeated opening, then turn the relationship into a natural request.

Check that the occasions are real and relevant and that the request identifies its beneficiary and purpose.

## Limits and results
The author reports $40,000 raised by a 115-word letter, without accounts or comparative testing. This is unverified reporting. A familiar congregation differs from a cold audience; rhetoric alone cannot be credited with the amount raised.

Evidence status: Source-reported result; not independently verified.

## English writing craft

**Original fictional fundraising practice:** “It held your first sketches, your late revisions, your finished plans. Give the next designer a place to begin.”

The three noun phrases progress through a working process instead of repeating a synonym. “Your” ties each stage to the reader. “The next designer” then turns remembered benefit into a future beneficiary. The source’s repeated “It gives” demonstrates anaphora; this practice instead repeats a possessive within a three-part sequence.
