# Let visible behavior carry feeling | Showing vs. telling

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-vgc-showing-vs-telling.md) · Source case and author/publisher account

## Source observations
David Abbott’s Chivas Regal Father’s Day ad expresses gratitude through everyday scenes before connecting them to a gift of whisky. The lesson’s comparisons replace abstract qualities with actions or objects. The author reads the ad differently as a son and as a father, emphasizing the memories readers supply.

## Interpretation and application
Give enough detail to picture an action without explaining every circumstance. Connect the feeling to a plausible product action. Use when words such as thoughtful or reliable leave the meaning invisible.

1. Find a real action or object behind each abstract judgment.
2. Remove unnecessary background and connect the scene to a relevant next step.

Check whether a reader can picture the moment and whether the product connection holds.

## Limits and results
Family experience varies; nostalgia may evoke loss. Emotional intensity does not establish purchasing effects. The evidence concerns the author’s response and the visible creative; sales and conversion are unknown.

Evidence status: Effectiveness unknown.

## English writing craft

**Original fictional service exercise:** “Before the first customer arrived, she had already set a dry towel beside every wet umbrella.”

The supplied practice contains a timing ambiguity: whose umbrellas were wet before customers arrived? A clearer revision is: “As each customer shook out a wet umbrella, she held out a dry towel.” The matched “wet” and “dry” make the response visible. Concrete detail still needs coherent timing; vividness alone is not enough.
