# Scale-weight inquiries: turn an overlooked specification into a decision-ready answer

[English case index](../../README.en.md) · [简体中文](../C-sugarman-scale-buyer-questions.md) · Source case and author/publisher account

- **Context:** a scale or balance; the Chinese source on p. 127 does not specifically identify a bathroom weight-loss scale.
- **Reader:** mail-order buyers who have seen the ad but need to assess fit.
- **Task:** identify missing decision information through real calls.
- **Medium:** ordering calls and print-ad feedback.

## What the material shows

- Sugarman says he answered toll-free order calls himself to hear buying concerns.
- The ad showed a scale on the floor and had also shown it held in a hand. It gave capacity but omitted the unit's own weight. Although the author had considered that unimportant, repeated questions led him to add it.
- Page 128 continues with physical properties. Later discussion of trial periods and price comparisons is separate; it does not report sales after adding weight.

## How the writing works

The selection criterion changes from what the seller regards as a selling point to what the buyer still needs to decide. Weight may affect handling, placement, or fit, but the book does not identify these customers' reasons. Repeated inquiries alone establish a need for an answer. A hand-held image suggests scale without providing a comparable figure; capacity and unit weight answer different questions.

Record the question's decision role, frequency, and answer source rather than adding every inquiry to body copy. Benefits explain why to want the product; specifications explain suitability; a comparison diagram can clarify quantities. Listening to calls reveals what remains unresolved after reading, beyond whether people like the ad. The author's suggestion that noncallers may have abandoned the purchase is a hypothesis, not measured loss.

## Writing steps to borrow

1. Record actual presale wording and distinguish recurring questions from exceptional requests.
2. Identify the decision each question may block and verify the reason with buyers.
3. Obtain the answer with units, definition, and applicable model.
4. Place important answers in the relevant scenario or specifications and observe changes in repeated inquiries and ordering.

## Draft checks

- Are capacity, supported load, and unit weight confused?
- Is an image being treated as a precise specification?
- Is improvement measured rather than assumed?

## Suitable tasks

- Remote buyers cannot handle the product, or repeatedly ask about specifications, compatibility, and conditions.

## Transfer limits

- Callers do not represent the whole market; noncallers cannot automatically be counted as lost because weight was missing.
- Keep this case separate from the chapter 17 bathroom-scale editing exercise.

## Reported results and evidence

The author reports many weight inquiries and adding the figure. No call sample, actual weight, or before/after sales comparison is supplied.

Evidence status: Results are reported or relayed by the source; not independently verified.

## English writing craft

**Original response exercise when the weight is not yet confirmed:** “I’ll confirm what the scale itself weighs and send you the figure.”

*Itself* distinguishes product weight from capacity without technical clutter. *Confirm* names the actual next action, and *the figure* specifies the deliverable. Do not replace an unavailable number with a guessed one or invent why the customer asked.
